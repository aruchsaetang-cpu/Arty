## Script (voiceover)

1. In 1945, five Navy bombers flew out over the ocean on a routine training flight. None of them came back. Then the plane sent to find them disappeared too.
2. On December 5th, Flight 19 took off from Fort Lauderdale, Florida, just after two in the afternoon.
3. Five TBM Avenger torpedo bombers, fourteen men, led by an experienced pilot, Lieutenant Charles Taylor.
4. It was a navigation exercise. Fly east, drop practice bombs, turn north, and come home. About two hours in the air.
5. [LOST] About an hour and a half in, Taylor radioed that both of his compasses were out.
6. He believed they were over the Florida Keys. They were probably over the Bahamas.
7. Other pilots and the base could hear him on the radio. They urged him to fly west, toward the setting sun.
8. Trying to get home, the flight may have turned the wrong way, further out over the Atlantic. Night was falling. The weather was getting worse.
9. One of the last messages heard: when the first plane drops below ten gallons, we all go down together.
10. [THE SEARCH] A PBM Mariner flying boat took off to search for them, with thirteen men aboard.
11. Soon after, a ship reported flames on the water. The Mariner was never heard from again.
12. Six aircraft. Twenty seven men. In one evening.
13. A huge air and sea search followed for days. It found nothing. No wreckage. No bodies.
14. The Navy first blamed Lieutenant Taylor. His family fought the ruling, and it was changed.
15. [THE THEORIES] The likeliest answer: lost and out of fuel, the planes ditched in rough seas at night. But the Navy's final report listed the cause as unknown.
16. Divers have since found Avenger wrecks off Florida. None has ever been confirmed as Flight 19.
17. And the story helped create a legend: the Bermuda Triangle.
