## Intent

The 2:30 version of Hollow Truth File 007: D.B. Cooper. A torch searches a wall of
evidence; each passage of the voiceover lands on one piece, a red string pulled to the next.
Chapter cards split it into the case, what happened, the clues and the theories. It ends on the
whole wall and "WHO WAS HE?"

## Script (voiceover)

1. In 1971, a man hijacked a passenger jet, collected a fortune in ransom, and jumped out into a freezing storm at night. He has never been identified.
2. Northwest Orient Flight 305, from Portland to Seattle, the day before Thanksgiving.
3. A quiet man in a dark suit handed a flight attendant a note. It said he had a bomb in his briefcase.
4. His ticket said Dan Cooper. A news mix-up later made it D.B. Cooper.
5. He ordered a bourbon and soda, paid for it, and tried to tip the flight attendant. Witnesses said he was calm, even polite.
6. [THE DEMANDS] He wanted $200,000, and four parachutes.
7. In Seattle, he got the money and the parachutes, and let the passengers go.
8. For two hours, the plane circled while the FBI gathered the cash: ten thousand $20 bills, every serial number recorded.
9. Then he ordered the plane back into the air, toward Mexico City, flying low and slow.
10. [THE JUMP] Somewhere over southwest Washington, he lowered the rear stairs. At 8:13 PM, the crew felt the tail jolt. He was gone.
11. He jumped into darkness and rain, over thick forest, in a business suit and loafers.
12. In 1980, an 8-year-old boy found almost $6,000 of the ransom in a riverbank. The serial numbers matched.
13. Over the years, the FBI looked at more than 800 suspects. None was ever confirmed.
14. [THE THEORIES] The FBI believed he probably didn't survive the jump.
15. One of the reserve parachutes he was given was a training dummy, sewn shut. Some think that means he was no expert jumper.
16. But his body, his parachute, and nearly all of the money were never found.
17. In 2016, the FBI stopped actively investigating. It's still the only unsolved airplane hijacking in American history.
