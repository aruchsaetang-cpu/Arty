## Script (voiceover)

1. In 1590, an English governor came back to his colony in America, and found it empty. More than a hundred people, gone. And one word carved into a post.
2. In 1587, about 115 settlers landed on Roanoke Island, in what is now North Carolina.
3. Among them was the governor's newborn granddaughter, Virginia Dare, the first English child born in the Americas.
4. And this was England's second try. An earlier colony had already given up and gone home, and fifteen men left behind to hold the island had vanished.
5. [THE LONG WAIT] The colony was short on supplies, so governor John White sailed back to England for more.
6. White didn't want to go. The colonists insisted. He was the one person who could convince England to send help.
7. But England went to war with Spain. Ships were held back to fight the Spanish Armada. White was stuck.
8. It took him three years to get back.
9. [WHAT HE FOUND] He landed in August 1590, on his granddaughter's third birthday. The houses had been taken down. There was no sign of a battle, and no bodies.
10. Carved into a post was one word: Croatoan. On a tree nearby, three letters: C, R, O.
11. Before he left, White had told them: if you leave in danger, carve a cross. There was no cross.
12. His own chests had been dug up and broken open. His books and maps lay ruined by the rain.
13. Croatoan was a nearby island, and the name of its people. But a storm drove White's ships away. He never got to search it.
14. [THE THEORIES] Many historians think the settlers went to Croatoan Island, and joined the people there.
15. Archaeologists on Hatteras Island, the old Croatoan, have found English objects from the right era. Some think that's the answer.
16. Others think they moved inland. Digs have turned up English artifacts, but nothing that settles it.
17. Or they tried to sail home, or were attacked, and died. More than four hundred years later, no one knows for sure.
