## Script (voiceover)

1. There's a book in a library at Yale that no one on Earth can read. It's around six hundred years old, and every page is a mystery.
2. It's called the Voynich manuscript, after Wilfrid Voynich, a rare book dealer who bought it in 1912.
3. Today it's kept at Yale's rare book library.
4. Its known history goes back to seventeenth century Prague. A letter claims an emperor, Rudolf the Second, once paid six hundred gold ducats for it.
5. [THE PAGES] It has about two hundred and forty pages of vellum, covered in writing and drawings.
6. Many pages show plants. But most of them don't match any plant botanists have ever identified.
7. Others show circular star charts and signs of the zodiac.
8. And some show women bathing in green pools, connected by strange tubes.
9. There's even a huge fold-out page, a map of circles, linked by strange paths and towers.
10. [THE SCRIPT] The writing flows smoothly, with almost no corrections. But it's in a script that appears nowhere else.
11. Some words repeat two or three times in a row, something that's rare in real languages.
12. Top codebreakers, including some who cracked enemy codes in World War Two, tried to read it. They failed.
13. In 2009, carbon dating showed the vellum was made in the early 1400s.
14. And statistical studies show its words follow patterns found in real languages.
15. [THE THEORIES] Maybe it's a code, hiding a known language. Or a real language, written in an invented alphabet.
16. Or maybe it's an elaborate fake, meaningless text made to look important. If so, it has fooled people for centuries.
17. Over the years, many people have announced they've solved it. Every time, other experts have rejected the claim.
18. Either way, no one has ever proven they can read a single sentence.
